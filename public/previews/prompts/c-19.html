The build, in five parts

How a frame gets made

Nine tubes, one drawing, nine joints and eleven days: what happens between your fitting and the first ride, told by the person who does it.

By Tom Aldred, who has built 312 of themKelham Island, Sheffield8 minute read

Motion is reduced, so every part of the story is shown complete and the drawing stays finished.

01 · Day 1

The tubing arrives straight, and stays that way

Every frame starts as nine lengths of Columbus Spirit, cold-drawn in Milan and butted to 0.7 mm at the ends and 0.4 mm in the middle. They come in a cardboard tube, and the first job is to roll each one on a granite plate. About one set in thirty goes back.

02 · Days 2–3

The measurement we will not move on

Before anything is cut, the frame is drawn full size from your fitting: inseam, torso, reach, and how you actually ride. The angles come out of that drawing, not a size chart. For most riders it lands near a 72.5° head angle and a 73.5° seat angle, with the bottom bracket 70 mm below the axles.

Once you have signed the drawing, it does not change.

scale-fade

03 · Days 4–6

Brazing is a temperature, not a technique

Each joint is fillet-brazed with silver-bearing bronze at about 870 °C: hot enough for the filler to flow, cool enough that a 0.7 mm wall keeps its strength. Too hot and the tube goes soft and grey; too cool and the fillet sits on top instead of wetting in. The seat cluster takes longest, about forty minutes.

04 · Days 7–10

Paint is the last thing that can go wrong

The frame is shot-blasted, phosphated and sprayed in two coats of Moor Green, with clear lacquer over hand-painted lettering. It cures for three days before anyone touches it.

A run or a speck of grit means stripping it back to bare steel. That happens about once a month.

05 · Day 11

Eleven days later

It is built up with your wheels, bars and saddle, checked for alignment to half a millimetre, and ridden fifty kilometres round the Peak before it is boxed. It ships with the full-size drawing, signed, and a lifetime frame guarantee for the first owner.
