The 12 weeks

Most people quit in week three. Here is what happens instead.

Nobody gets a dramatic result in a fortnight, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. The block is built so weeks one to three are deliberately easy to finish — that is the part where people normally fall off.

Weeks 1–3

Assess & groove

Movement screen, a starting number on six lifts, and technique work at loads you can absolutely handle. Nothing here should be hard.

Weeks 4–7

Build

Volume climbs. Same six lifts, more sets, small jumps each week. This is where the work actually accumulates.

Weeks 8–10

Intensify

Fewer sets, heavier top sets, conditioning gets shorter and sharper. Sleep and food start mattering more than the programme.

Weeks 11–12

Test & deload

Re-test the same six lifts and the same measurements, then a week backing off. You leave with a number, not a feeling.

What we actually measure

Every fortnight, the same five things, taken the same way, at the same time of day. You get the spreadsheet. If a number is not moving after six weeks, we change the programme rather than the story about the programme.

  • Six working liftsSquat, hinge, press, pull, carry, row — load × reps
  • Waist and hipTape, morning, before food
  • Body weight trendWeekly average, never a single day
  • Resting heart rateOn waking, from your own watch
  • Sessions completedOut of 36. The one that predicts the rest
  • Sleep, self-reportedHours and a 1–5 quality score

The coach

Thirteen years, one room, no franchise behind me

I started on the floor of a 24-hour gym in Coburg in 2013 handing out free assessments nobody wanted. I now coach out of one room at Ironbark in Brunswick East, and I cap it at eight one-to-one clients because past that I stop remembering what happened in your last session.

Most of the people I work with are between 35 and 60, have trained on and off for years, and have something that hurts. The job is usually not motivation. It is picking the right five exercises and getting you to do them for long enough to matter.

If your goal is a stage, a photo shoot or a number on the scale by a fixed date, I am probably the wrong coach and I will say so in the consult.

Questions

Before you book

What actually happens in the free consultation?

Thirty minutes. I ask what you have tried, what hurt, and what your week genuinely looks like — not what you wish it looked like. You do a short movement screen if we are in the gym. At the end I tell you which block fits, or that none of them do. Nobody is asked for a card.

I have not trained in years. Am I too unfit to start?

No. Weeks one to three exist precisely for this. The first month is loads you can do comfortably for the stated reps — if you finish a session feeling wrecked in the first fortnight, I have programmed it wrong.

Will I lose a specific amount of weight?

I will not put a number on it, and I would be suspicious of anyone who does. What I will commit to is that we measure the same six lifts and five metrics every fortnight and change the plan when they stall. What your body does with that depends on sleep, food, stress and genetics I do not control.

What if I miss a session?

Give me 12 hours and we move it, no charge. Less than 12 hours and it is used, because the slot is yours and I cannot fill it. Every block includes one pause of up to three weeks for illness or travel.

Do I need a gym membership as well?

Yes, for the days you train on your own. Ironbark is $22 a week on a month-to-month and there is no joining fee through me. Your sessions with me are covered by the block price.

Can I claim it on private health?

Personal training is not claimable. If a physio or exercise physiologist refers you under a care plan, that part may be — bring the paperwork to the consult and I will work with them directly.

Start with a conversation, not a contract

I take eight one-to-one clients at a time and two of those slots open in March. If they fill, the consult is still free and I will tell you honestly when the next one is — or point you at someone who has room now.
