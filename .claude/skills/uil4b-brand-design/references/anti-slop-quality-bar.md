# Anti-slop quality bar

## Definition

"AI slop" is the felt result of an interface that appears fluent and polished
but insufficiently authored for its product, audience, or content. It often
creates emotional distance: users may read it as interchangeable, inflated,
uncanny, overproduced, or untrustworthy.

Judge combinations and intent, not isolated techniques. No colour, type style,
card, gradient, animation, or layout is inherently slop.

## Diagnostic lenses

### Product specificity

**Warning signs**

- The headline could describe dozens of unrelated products.
- Decorative product mock-ups contain implausible data or controls.
- Feature sections list capabilities without showing inputs, decisions, or
  outputs.
- The page talks about transformation while hiding the actual workflow.

**Correction**

Expose UIL4B-specific work: system inputs, validation, iteration, saved projects,
export artefacts, meaningful constraints, and the relationship between Workspace,
Discover, and Learn.

### Composition

**Warning signs**

- The page follows a stock sequence without a content reason.
- Every idea is enclosed in an equally weighted rounded card.
- Floating panels, orbiting chips, and disconnected UI fragments imply activity
  but provide no readable story.
- Large empty areas create spectacle while obscuring the next action.

**Correction**

Let content determine the structure. Vary density, grouping, and pacing according
to the user's decision. Use containment only where it expresses a real object,
boundary, state, or interaction.

### Visual language

**Warning signs**

- Glow, blur, glass, gradients, shadows, grain, or oversized display type are
  layered together as a generic premium signal.
- Pills are used for navigation, metadata, actions, and decoration until roles
  become indistinguishable.
- Icons or abstract shapes fill space without strengthening recognition.
- The screen relies on one fashionable motif instead of a hierarchy.

**Correction**

Assign each visual technique a job. Preserve enough restraint that signature
choices remain noticeable. Prefer a small number of repeatable, meaningful
motifs over a collection of fashionable effects.

### Language and proof

**Warning signs**

- Copy relies on "effortless", "supercharge", "reimagine", "at scale", or
  similarly vague claims without evidence.
- Metrics, customer quotes, logos, scarcity, or urgency are fabricated or weakly
  sourced.
- Headings sound polished but do not help the user predict the product.

**Correction**

Name the user's task, constraint, output, or changed state. Demonstrate value
with real product behaviour and honest limits. Use proof only when it is true,
current, and attributable.

### Interaction and motion

**Warning signs**

- Everything animates because motion is available.
- Hover effects carry more personality than task feedback.
- Scroll choreography delays understanding.
- Loading effects imply intelligence without explaining system state.

**Correction**

Use motion to preserve continuity, show causality, communicate status, or reward
a completed action. Keep core information and controls available without
choreography. Honour reduced-motion preferences.

### System coherence

**Warning signs**

- A new page introduces its own visual universe.
- Similar controls change treatment without semantic reason.
- Spacing, hierarchy, and state behaviour feel generated section by section.
- Marketing promises an experience the app does not continue.

**Correction**

Reuse tokens and behavioural patterns intentionally. Make deviations explicit
and test whether they should become accepted brand knowledge or remain local.

## Emotional review

Do not ask only whether a screen is attractive. Ask what it makes a visually
literate user infer:

- **Trust:** Does it feel honest about capability and status?
- **Agency:** Does the user feel in control or merely impressed at?
- **Recognition:** Is there a memorable UIL4B logic beyond the logo?
- **Clarity:** Can the user predict the next action and result?
- **Care:** Do edge states and small transitions feel considered?
- **Restraint:** Is emphasis scarce enough to remain meaningful?

Capture the user's own emotional language verbatim when possible, then translate
it into testable design causes. Keep the quote and the interpretation separate.

## Review verdict

Return one of:

- **Distinctive and coherent**: clearly UIL4B, useful, and internally consistent.
- **Strong but borrowed**: usable and polished, but signature logic is weak.
- **Overproduced**: effects or rhetoric outrun product evidence.
- **Under-authored**: functional but generic, default-like, or inconsistent.
- **Misaligned**: communicates the wrong product, audience, or emotional promise.

Pair the verdict with the three highest-impact corrections. Do not produce a
long taste inventory.
